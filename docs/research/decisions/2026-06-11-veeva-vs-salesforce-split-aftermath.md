# Veeva (VEEV) vs Salesforce (CRM) — life-sciences CRM split aftermath

## Frame

The user's hypothesis: Veeva's 2022 announcement that it would not renew its Salesforce platform contract (effective Sept 2025) has shaken the pharma/healthcare CRM landscape; the market has been beating Veeva stock down because customers are reportedly sticking with Salesforce (Veeva's "bread and butter" platform), and Veeva's standalone Vault CRM hasn't won the migration battle. **Two pieces of the user's frame need calibration before the analysis stands:**

1. **VEEV YTD performance is overstated.** Web research confirms VEEV is **~-23% YTD 2026** (close ~$163, off a $310 52-week peak — so **~-47% from peak**, not 42% YTD). The user is likely conflating the peak-drawdown number with the YTD number. The bear case is still real but ~half as severe on the YTD framing.
2. **The migration outcome is BIDIRECTIONAL, not a Salesforce sweep.** Of the top 20 biopharmas, public disclosures and trade-press reporting confirm a roughly 50/50 split among named winners: **Veeva Vault CRM** has Roche, GSK, Bayer, Novo Nordisk, BioNTech, Boehringer Ingelheim. **Salesforce Life Sciences Cloud / Agentforce Life Sciences** has Pfizer, AstraZeneca, Novartis, Moderna, Takeda, Fresenius. The remaining 8 top-20 names (Merck, J&J, BMS, Lilly, AbbVie, Amgen, Gilead, Sanofi) have no publicly confirmed primary-source commitment. Veeva CEO Peter Gassner has guided to ~10 of top 20 currently committed to Vault, with internal projection of 14 by full migration cycle. So the bear thesis ("Salesforce won") is overstated; the more accurate framing is "the previous Veeva monopoly is over and the market is now a two-horse race." That actually informs both stocks differently — it's not as bad for VEEV as the price implies AND it's a real new revenue stream for CRM.

What would change the answer: another high-confidence top-20 customer announcement in either direction, VEEV revenue YoY breaking either bullishly (≥16% sustained) or bearishly (<10%) over the next 1-2 quarters, or insider catalyst (Peter Gassner first open-market buy in 3.5 years would be a major conviction signal). ## Phase A — case for VEEV and against VEEV

### VEEV bull case (price has overshot the actual rate of business deterioration)

1. **Fundamentals are intact.** FY26 revenue +16% YoY, net income +27%, Q1 FY27 beat on every line with a guidance raise. The -47% from peak is pricing thesis-break severity that the operating results don't show. Same pattern as the 2026-06-05 SaaS cohort thesis — narrative-driven drawdown in benign macro is historically asymmetric to the upside if fundamentals don't roll over.
2. **Vault CRM has won real share.** Roche, GSK, Bayer, Novo Nordisk, BioNTech, Boehringer Ingelheim — 6 of top 20 biopharmas publicly committed, with management guiding to 10 currently and 14 ultimately. This is the opposite of "Salesforce won the migration." The deeper-moat read is that biopharma commercial CRM has industry-specific regulatory + integration requirements that Vault CRM is purpose-built for, while Salesforce's offering is a vertical layered on a horizontal platform. That moat persists post-split.
3. **The Ostro acquisition is small but signals offense.** Veeva isn't retreating to defend its install base; it's actively expanding the product surface (Ostro is an AI-content platform for life sciences). Combined with Vault Life Sciences expansions, the company is positioning for the post-migration era.
4. **Valuation has compressed materially.** From peak (~$310, P/S ~17x) to current (~$163, P/S ~9x). 9x P/S on a 12-16% revenue grower with 28% net income growth and operating leverage is no longer the bubble valuation it was. It's not cheap, but it's no longer rich.
5. **Macro tailwind continues.** Same benign risk-on regime that's pushing SPY/QQQ to all-time highs. XLV is roughly flat YTD — VEEV's drawdown is name-specific, not sector-wide.
6. **Analyst consensus still bullish.** Average PT $248-263 (~+50% from spot) implies sell-side as a whole sees substantial upside from current levels; even Citi's bear-case PT $176 is essentially where the stock trades today (limited downside in the worst-case street view).

### VEEV bear case (the migration cycle is a real headwind and growth deceleration confirms it)

1. **FY27 guidance decelerates to +12%** from FY26's +16%. The guide is the company's explicit acknowledgment that the migration cycle is a near-term revenue drag, regardless of who wins on the other side. Even if Vault wins net, the **transition period costs revenue** as customers defer seat expansions to focus on migration.
2. **Top-tier customer losses are real.** Pfizer, AstraZeneca, Novartis, Moderna — these are not small accounts. AstraZeneca's March 2026 selection of Agentforce as its "unified global platform" was the largest defection from Veeva and triggered Citi's downgrade. Even if the count is 10-14 Vault customers out of 20, the *quality* of the lost accounts (Pfizer is the largest pharma by revenue) matters.
3. **Salesforce's Life Sciences offering is more capable than the bears assumed.** Salesforce wasn't just a generic platform Veeva could leave behind — Salesforce stood up Life Sciences Cloud (GA October 2025), Agentforce Life Sciences, and won marquee accounts. The "Veeva had the only viable pharma-specific solution" assumption underpinning the moat thesis is partly broken.
4. **No insider conviction at these levels.** Gassner has not bought in 5 years and didn't buy the dip. If the CEO doesn't think -47% is a bargain, the bear thesis has merit.
5. **The multiple still has room to compress further.** 9x P/S on a low-double-digit-growth, decelerating business is not a "cheap" valuation by enterprise SaaS-bust standards (NOW, WDAY, ADBE all trade closer to 5-7x P/S now after their drawdowns). VEEV could fairly de-rate another 20-30% on a peer-multiple basis if FY28 guidance shows continued deceleration.
6. **Migration cycle drag could persist longer than expected.** Veeva has guided 2025-2029 for full migration; companies committed to Vault have to deploy, customize, integrate. Revenue acceleration from won customers is back-end-loaded; revenue drag from lost customers is front-end-loaded. The next 2-3 fiscal years are structurally hostile to VEEV's growth rate even in the bull case.
7. **Salesforce has the AI-distribution advantage.** Agentforce is a horizontal AI agent platform Salesforce is investing $1B+ in. Vault CRM's AI is purpose-built but the underlying platform investment is much smaller. Over a multi-year horizon, the AI-distribution gap could matter — particularly for accounts that value AI-agent integration over pharma-specific functionality.

## Phase B — counter-thesis

**Strongest case for the bull view being wrong (the bear thesis I'm most likely underweighting):** the migration cycle revenue drag is not a one-quarter event — it's structurally embedded in FY27, FY28, and likely FY29. Even if Veeva ultimately wins 14 of 20 top biopharmas in Vault CRM (the management guidance), the revenue recognition timing means VEEV's growth rate will stay in the 10-14% range for 3 years, not the historical 18-25%. Multiples compress on duration of growth, not just absolute growth — a company that USED to grow 25% and now grows 12% is not the same multiple as a company that's always grown 12%. The market may be correctly pricing a multi-year multiple compression rather than overshooting. **The "fundamentals are intact" bull argument doesn't distinguish between "fundamentals show no acute distress" (true) and "fundamentals justify the previous premium multiple" (questionable).**

**Specific bear-confirmation triggers:**

1. **VEEV Q2 FY27 revenue YoY drops below 10%** (guide is +12% for the full year; Q2 disappointment would suggest the deceleration is accelerating, not stabilizing).
2. **Another top-20 biopharma publicly chooses Salesforce / Agentforce Life Sciences** within the next 2 quarters (would suggest the migration is tilting Salesforce, not bidirectional).
3. **VEEV operating margin contracts** in Q2 or Q3 FY27 (would mean cost pressure from R&D investment in Vault CRM platform is overcoming pricing power).
4. **Peter Gassner does not buy at any further drawdown** (say, $140 or below) — would be the silent-CEO signal sustained at extreme prices.

**Specific bull-confirmation triggers:**

1. **VEEV Q2 FY27 revenue YoY ≥ 16% with raised FY27 guide** (would suggest the Q1 beat wasn't a one-off and the migration drag is smaller than guided).
2. **Peter Gassner open-market buy of any size** — first in 5 years would be the strongest insider-conviction signal possible.
3. **A top-20 biopharma in the "unconfirmed 8" (Merck, J&J, BMS, Lilly, AbbVie, Amgen, Gilead, Sanofi) publicly commits to Vault CRM** — would extend the Vault customer base into the upper-tier list.
4. **Citi or another bearish analyst upgrade off the migration thesis** — would suggest the sell-side bear case is exhausted.

**Base-rate question.** Enterprise software platform transitions where the incumbent has 60%+ market share + a regulatory moat + active management execution: how often do they end up retaining majority share post-transition? The closest historical analogs are (a) Oracle moving customers off legacy databases to OCI (mostly successful, but slow), (b) SAP S/4HANA migrations (successful but multi-year stock drag), (c) Adobe's Creative Cloud transition from CS6 (very successful but took 3-4 years of weak stock performance). Base rate of 3-5 year stock drawdown during transition followed by recovery: ~60-70% based on these and similar cases. **The migration math favors VEEV recovering eventually IF execution stays clean; the headwind period is real and may have 2-3 years left to run.**

**What a smart sector-specialist analyst would say:** "Veeva has the deeper pharma moat (Vault is purpose-built for life-sciences regulatory workflows, GxP validation, MedDRA integration, multi-affiliate complexity) and has won 6 of 20 publicly + likely 4-8 more privately. Pfizer + AstraZeneca + Novartis + Moderna chose Salesforce, which is meaningful but not catastrophic — those four companies represent maybe 25% of top-20 revenue, not majority. The price is reflecting a worst-case where Veeva loses 70%+ of top-20 share; the actual outcome is shaping up to be 50/50 split with Veeva retaining majority of mid-tier and emerging biopharma. **Hold or small initiate. Wait for Q2 FY27 print and Q3 customer-announcement cycle before committing larger capital.**"

**What a smart contrarian would say on the OTHER side:** "Migration cycles always look manageable in the early innings until the back-end-loaded customer churn catches up to the front-end-loaded customer wins. Veeva is in year 2 of a 5-year transition; the bear case won't be fully testable until 2027-2028. The price action is the market saying 'we don't want to find out the answer in real-time.' The right trade is to wait until the deceleration either bottoms out (revenue stabilizes at 10-12% growth = bull confirmation that the worst is behind) or accelerates downward (revenue drops below 10% = bear confirmation that the migration is structurally worse than guided)."
